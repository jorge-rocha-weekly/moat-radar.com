ABOUT MOAT RADAR

What Moat Radar Is

Methodology, scope and editorial criteria

Every week, Moat Radar covers what mattered most for the AI market over the past few days, through two fixed lenses. Supply covers who builds, finances and sells the infrastructure: hyperscalers, semiconductors, capex. Demand covers who the end consumer chooses, uses and loves: the models and products that become habits.

This newsletter has three layers of depth, each for a different kind of reader. "In One Line" is the summary, for anyone who just wants to catch up on the week's themes. "The Dilemma Map" walks through each dilemma more slowly, for anyone who wants to understand what it is about. And the Supply and Demand sections, further down in each edition, are the deep dive: the data, dates and examples that support each dilemma, for anyone who wants to go deeper.

Beyond original insights gathered by cross-referencing multiple sources (what we call "below the radar"), we also cover off-market deals, institutional moves, executive transactions, business noise and innovations still emerging or in early stages, before they become headlines.

Curation cross-checks multiple market sources, already split along the same supply and demand lens, plus a cross-cutting read covering both sides day to day. The source hierarchy prioritizes primary documents (company reports, regulatory filings, original papers), followed by specialist analysis, journalistic reporting and, only when there is no alternative, aggregators.

Written by Jorge Rocha, with nothing for sale, no investment recommendation: a personal reading of the market, shared openly.

The seven dilemmas

Moat Radar's core framework organizes the AI market into seven dilemmas, four on the demand side and three on the supply side:

On the demand side: model vs. channel (who will win the end consumer); the corporate dilemma (who converts AI into results); and the humanoid race (when this technology enters daily life).

On the supply side: capex vs. bubble (whether the pace of investment is sustainable); the bottleneck economy (who profits from chip and memory shortages); concentration and verticalization (dependence on a handful of suppliers); and how long we will keep control (the most basic question of all, about the security and governance of AI systems).

References that shape us

Beyond each week's own curation, Moat Radar's reading is shaped by a fixed set of voices we follow closely: Stratechery (Ben Thompson), SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel), One Useful Thing (Ethan Mollick), Axios AI+ and the Dwarkesh Podcast. They are not our only input, but they are the recurring reference behind the tone and the themes we prioritize.
