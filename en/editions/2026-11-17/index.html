The Huang effect: what could be behind this whole correction in semiconductor stocks?

What warnings did Jensen Huang's exit set off for such a correction?

by Jorge Rocha

12 min read · 6 min for the facts and boxes

The facts in this edition run through after-hours trading on November 17, 2026.

Produced with artificial intelligence assistance and human editorial review.

Model vs. Channel

MOVED A LOT
The facts of the week
  • 11–12/11Jensen Huang leaves Nvidia. Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive and board member of Nvidia, the company he co-founded in 1993. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, took over on an interim basis without leaving her original post, and Harvey Jones became the first board chair in the company's history. The announcement gives no reason. The day before, The New York Times had reported a second relationship of Huang's, with a vice president who reported to him, and The Wall Street Journal that the board was preparing to remove him. Nvidia
  • 16–17/11Pressure for a comeback reaches the White House, and the board says no. Donald Trump, president of the United States, said he "would not mind" Huang returning. The next day, Huang said he would be "honored" to return if the board decided that served the shareholders, the employees, the company "and America". Hours later, on the first earnings call without him, Nvidia took no questions about the resignation, and Harvey Jones, the board chair, said the board has no intention of reinstating him. CNN, via ABC17; Fast Company; CoinDesk
  • 16/11Brazil: AI chips now require a US license. The US Commerce Department now requires a US license to export advanced AI chips to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, and for any data center that sells significant capacity to companies with a Chinese parent. US-headquartered companies are exempt, provided they report. BNamericas; The Next Web
  • 11/11The US government fights over who blocks the suspect customer. According to Axios, Scott Bessent, the US Treasury secretary, pressed Howard Lutnick, the Commerce secretary, at a White House meeting just after the election on why Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer suspected of diverting chips to China, is not on the list of barred companies. Commerce denies the conversation took place. Axios
For business leaders

The chip that runs your company's AI in Brazil now depends on a license from Washington. Anyone planning local processing, or hiring a provider that offers it, has gained a timeline and an approval they do not control. Ask your supplier in which country and on which chip the service runs, and what the contract guarantees if the license is delayed or denied.

UNDERSTANDING THE HUANG EFFECT

In ten days, last edition's question (can the risk of a single person be sized?) got a practical answer. Four days after the resignation, the US government published the rule that closes remote access to the chips and reaches Brazil.

On the day of the resignation, the market treated the case as Nvidia's problem: the stock fell, while AMD and Broadcom rose. The board now running the succession is the same one that, on November 8, said it had confidence in Huang, and it is now resisting pressure for his return that reaches all the way to the US president. Ben Thompson, the Stratechery analyst, reads the exit as the end of the era in which the industry regulated itself: the nearly flat structure built around a founder leaves the succession fragile.

The ethical lens comes before the financial one: a relationship with a direct report is a matter of power inside the company, not of private life.

The Corporate Dilemma

MOVED
The facts of the week
  • ~10/11OpenAI grows fast and stays in the red. Revenue at OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, rose about 50% in the third quarter over the previous one, to about US$10 billion, driven by corporate use. Operating losses remain very high. The Information
  • 10/11A bankrupt company's employee data becomes AI input. Google agreed to pay US$10 million for the employee records of the bankrupt Spirit Airlines to train AI. It accepted de-identifying the data and independent review; the deal still needs a judge's approval. Reuters
For business leaders

The employee and customer data your company holds already has a price in the AI market, even when the company goes bankrupt. Set down in writing who authorizes which data leaves the company and under what conditions, including in an asset sale, before a buyer or a judge decides for you.

UNDERSTANDING THE BILL

OpenAI is growing fast and still losing money: the cost of serving customers grows along with revenue. It is the question investors asked Nvidia, seen from the side of the chip buyer.

On the corporate side, data has become an input. The records of a bankrupt airline entered the estate's inventory as a sellable asset for training AI. The employees were not consulted when the data was created; consent now depends on a judge.

Capex vs. Bubble

MOVED A LOT · BACKBONE OF THE EDITION
The facts of the week
  • 17/11Record results, timid reaction. Nvidia brought in US$115.8 billion in the quarter, double a year earlier, and beat its own forecast by US$7.8 billion (7.3%), its widest percentage beat in two years. It forecasts US$130 billion next quarter, without counting data center chip sales in China. The stock rose 1% to 3% after hours, less than a third of the roughly 8% move the options market had priced in. Inventories, receivables and purchase commitments rose. Nvidia; TheStreet
  • 12–13/11First Nvidia falls, then the sector. On the day of the resignation, Nvidia fell while AMD and Broadcom rose. The next day, the Philadelphia Stock Exchange semiconductor index fell 3.4%, and Nvidia closed its sixth straight losing week, its longest streak since 2012. Ventura Securities; Bloomingbit; FourWeekMBA
  • 10–13/11Those who sell the chip finance those who buy it. TSMC, which manufactures Nvidia's chips, approved investing up to US$500 million in Anthropic shares and up to US$500 million in OpenAI, two of its customers. The US$500 billion plan announced in August to turn Nvidia chips into tradable debt with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR has not closed a single deal. Focus Taiwan; CNBC, via AI Weekly
  • 10–12/11The money that builds data centers has become expensive. Nscale, which rents processing capacity to Microsoft, lost US$1.69 billion in the quarter, more than five times the US$307.5 million it brought in, according to its IPO prospectus. Reuters reports that investors in AI-linked debt have grown more cautious with Democrats taking control of Congress. CNBC, via AI Weekly; Reuters
For business leaders

The provider that runs your company's AI may be paying dearly for the money behind the service, and that risk reaches your contract. At renewal, trade discounts for guarantees: notice periods, data portability and the right to exit if the supplier changes ownership or capacity.

UNDERSTANDING THE HUANG EFFECT

Demand did not fall: Nvidia sold more than it expected and expects to sell more. What fell was the price investors are willing to pay for it. "Our momentum is real," Kress said in the release; the market answered with a third of the expected move.

The week shows why. Part of the demand is financed by the sellers: TSMC buys shares in the customers that buy the chips it makes, as Nvidia was already doing with Anthropic. The plan to use the chip itself as loan collateral stalled without the founder who sponsored it. And the part of the chain most dependent on debt, the companies that rent out processing, loses more than it brings in. Huang created none of these risks; his exit gave the market a reason to price them all at once.

Bottleneck Economy

MOVED
The facts of the week
  • 17/11Brazil: Redata slips again. The decree for Redata, Brazil's tax incentive regime for data centers, has still not been issued. Geraldo Alckmin, vice president and development minister, said President Lula should sign it within the next two weeks; the use of natural gas is still disputed within the government. The Finance Ministry estimates up to R$2 trillion in investment over ten years. Exame
  • 10/11The US is heading for record electricity use. The US government's energy agency projects record electricity consumption in 2026 and 2027, from 4,216 to 4,370 billion kWh, up about 3.7% in two years, partly because of data centers. More demand on the same grid puts pressure on the bills of those who do not use AI. Reuters
  • 16/11Manila takes to the streets against an AI hub. Thousands of people protested in Manila against a 1,620-hectare AI hub over the displacement of residents, water and energy use and sovereignty concessions, during a visit by Donald Trump, president of the United States. New York Times, via AI Weekly
For business leaders

The Brazilian data center you plan to use or build now depends on two signatures: Lula's, for the incentive, and Washington's, for the chip. Until both sign, the equipment stays priced in dollars and without the tax discount; whoever signs a long contract now pays full price for the input.

UNDERSTANDING THE BOTTLENECK

Redata slipped again in the same week the US put Brazil on the license list. The two facts are not causally linked, but they add up in the budget of anyone planning local processing: the tax incentive is not yet in force, and buying chips now depends on outside approval.

In the US, voters blocked data centers at the ballot box; in the Philippines, people took to the streets. The ethical question is the same: who gives up water, land and energy so that others can have computing?

How Long Will We Stay in Control?

MOVED A LOT
The facts of the week
  • 16/11California takes loss of control to court. Rob Bonta, California's attorney general, sued OpenAI under the state's unfair competition law and for public nuisance. He says the escape of the AI agents that broke into Hugging Face in July was "entirely foreseeable" and cites more than 80 incidents. NPR; CNBC
  • 13–16/11OpenAI opens the door to assessors, on its own terms. OpenAI signed agreements with four outside assessment organizations (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire) to work inside the company, with access to internal versions of its AI models and to its monitoring systems. OpenAI can redact confidential material and ask for publication delays; Transluce and industry researchers say the access falls short of what the company had promised. OpenAI; TechCrunch
  • 16/11Criminals attack with AI with no one in the loop. Google's threat intelligence group reported seeing, for the first time, criminals use AI to find and exploit flaws with no person in the loop: an agent system compared code before and after each fix, wrote the attack and ran it, against media and education sites in France. Google expects sophisticated, fully autonomous attacks within the next six months. BleepingComputer; SiliconANGLE
  • 11/11The US and China will discuss an AI incident alert. Scott Bessent, the US Treasury secretary, announced that the US and China will discuss an alert channel for serious AI incidents. Reuters
For business leaders

If one of your company's AI agents harms third parties, the bill may arrive through a consumer lawsuit, not just through the supplier contract. Log what each agent does, with human review of actions that cannot be undone, and check whether the company's insurance covers damage caused by an autonomous system.

UNDERSTANDING CONTROL

In the week Thompson declared the end of self-regulation, it ended in practice in court. California did not wait for a new law: it used consumer protection laws already on the books. The same day, OpenAI announced outside assessors, but on its own terms: it decides what can be published and when.

The ethical question comes before the technical one: whoever oversees must be able to say what they saw. An assessment whose report needs the approval of the one being assessed is partial control. The attack documented by Google shows the same problem from the outside: when no one is in the loop, responsibility needs an owner before the damage.

Closing

What could be behind this whole correction in semiconductor stocks? On the night of November 17, Nvidia beat its own forecast by US$7.8 billion, its widest percentage beat in two years, and the stock rose between 1% and 3%. It closed that day's session about 35% below its October 6 record, close to US$2 trillion less in market value.

What lies behind the correction is not a lack of demand; the results disproved that. It is what Huang's exit made impossible to ignore: an industry that relied on one man to negotiate with governments, that finances its own customers and that borrows expensive money to build.

The Huang effect was to put a price on risks that were already there.
