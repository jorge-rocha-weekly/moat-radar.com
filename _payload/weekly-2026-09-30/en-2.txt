id="modelo-vs-canal"><h2 class="dilema-title">Model vs. Channel</h2><div class="mov">MOVED</div><div class="label-mono">The week's facts</div><ul class="facts-list"><li><span class="fact-date">05/10</span><b>OpenAI brings advertising inside ChatGPT.</b> The company will begin testing later in October, first in the U.S., visual ads shown during image generation. OpenAI says the ads will be clearly labeled and remain separate from the image being created. The company says ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion people each week.<span class="fact-src">OpenAI</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">30/09</span><b>U.S. court denies publishers antitrust protection against Google AI summaries.</b> Federal judge Amit Mehta dismissed lawsuits by education company Chegg and Penske Media, owner of Rolling Stone, over Google’s AI summaries. He ruled that an expectation of referral traffic in exchange for allowing content to be crawled is not an agreement protected by antitrust law, and that compensating publishers is a matter for legislators.<span class="fact-src">PYMNTS</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">05/10</span><b>AI subscriptions deliver more value than they charge for.</b> According to SemiAnalysis, the $200-a-month Claude subscription offers about five times the API-equivalent value of OpenAI’s comparable plan for the daily-driver workload it analyzed. Subscriptions would represent roughly 10% of Anthropic revenue while consuming more than 40% of its inference compute. OpenAI, meanwhile, cut the limits of its $200 plan in half and introduced a $500 tier.<span class="fact-src">SemiAnalysis</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">05/10</span><b>U.S. startup offers an open alternative to Chinese models.</b> Reflection, which received $800 million from Nvidia, released Beam, an open-weight AI model that companies can download, inspect and run on their own infrastructure. It is a U.S. bet against open models from Alibaba, DeepSeek and other Chinese labs.<span class="fact-src">Reflection via AI Business</span></li></ul><div class="callout"><div class="callout-label">For business leaders</div><p><b>If the AI assistant that brings the customer to you starts selling ads inside the answer, visibility becomes an auction.</b> Traffic that currently arrives free through search or recommendation gets a price, just as a U.S. court has ruled that antitrust law does not protect that flow. Media budget and AI-provider choice start becoming the same decision.</p></div><div class="label-mono" style="margin-top:30px;">UNDERSTANDING THE FACTS</div><div class="body-text"><p>The two largest AI labs have reached the same economics from opposite directions. According to SemiAnalysis, Anthropic gives away far more value in subscriptions than it charges for: subscriptions generate roughly 10% of revenue while using more than 40% of capacity. OpenAI cut usage on its most expensive plan and is now bringing ads deeper into the product. Cheap subscriptions do not pay for inference. The assistant’s revenue model starts converging with search: advertising.</p><p>Judge Mehta’s decision closes the loop from the other side. Publishers argued that Google broke an implicit bargain by answering with AI instead of sending readers to their sites. The judge acknowledged the economic harm but said antitrust law does not cover it. Businesses that depend on traffic from search engines or assistants have no legal floor: they either pay for visibility or negotiate directly with the channel owner.</p><p>Open weights are the escape route for companies that do not want to depend on that channel. Reflection is betting that Western companies will prefer a model they can inspect and run themselves over one they rent from a provider that also sells advertising.</p></div><div class="flags"><div class="flags-label">VISIBLE FLAGS</div><span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> The 1.2 billion weekly users figure is a metric published by OpenAI. <span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> Subscription figures are SemiAnalysis estimates; the same article also cites a roughly fourfold gap under another comparison.
