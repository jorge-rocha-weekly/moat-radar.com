class="dilema-block" id="dilema-1"><h2 class="dilema-title">Model vs. Channel</h2><div class="mov ">MOVED</div><p class="question">The question behind this dilemma: who owns the customer, the company that builds the AI model or the company that controls the path to it?</p><div class="label-mono">The facts this week</div><ul class="facts-list"><li><span class="fact-date">10/07</span>OpenAI began offering GPT-6, its newest AI model, to all ChatGPT users, including those on the free plan starting 10/08. Responses can now include ready-made charts, buttons and forms instead of text alone.<span class="fact-src">OpenAI, via VKTR</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">10/06–10/07</span>Elon Musk, chief executive of SpaceX, which owns the Grok assistant, announced that Grok Bot will route each task to the AI model best suited for it, even when that model belongs to a competitor: Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 for reasoning, Midjourney for image generation and Suno for music composition.<span class="fact-src">Elon Musk on X, via TeslaNorth and The Next Web</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">10/12</span>Microsoft is on track to pay Anthropic more than US$2 billion a year to use Claude in Copilot inside Word, Excel, Outlook and PowerPoint. The move was pushed by Charles Lamanna, the executive vice president responsible for Office, after customer complaints about Copilot; Anthropic's models performed better in Excel and PowerPoint.<span class="fact-src">The Information</span></li><li><span class="fact-date">10/10</span>Nvidia, which has already invested US$800 million in Reflection, a U.S. startup that builds AI models companies can download and run on their own computers, known as open-weight models, is discussing either acquiring the company or increasing its stake. In April, Reflection was seeking capital at a US$25 billion valuation. One structure under discussion is to hire the team and license the technology instead of buying the company.<span class="fact-src">Financial Times, via Reuters</span></li></ul><div class="callout"><div class="callout-label">For business leaders</div><p><b>If even Grok's owner and Microsoft switch AI models depending on the task, tying your operation to a single supplier has become the most expensive risk in the contract.</b> The company that controls the channel is treating the model as a replaceable component and uses that position to negotiate price. Contracts that do not let you switch models without rebuilding the connection to your systems put your company on the wrong side of that negotiation.</p></div><div class="label-mono">UNDERSTANDING THE CHANNEL</div><div class="body-text"><p>In the same week, two channel owners effectively said that the AI model is a component, not the product. Grok Bot will choose, for each request, the model that delivers the best result, including models from direct competitors. Microsoft, OpenAI's historic partner, will blend Claude into Office Copilot after customer complaints about results in Excel and PowerPoint. In both cases, whoever owns the customer decides which model serves the request, and the model maker becomes a supplier.</p><p>OpenAI is making the opposite move: it is bringing the entire product into its own channel. Giving the newest model to non-paying users as well keeps the user inside ChatGPT, where OpenAI controls the relationship. Nvidia is playing at the other end: by financing Reflection and discussing absorbing it, Nvidia secures an open-weight model that any company can run on its own infrastructure, aligned with the hardware Nvidia sells.</p><p>The emerging pattern is the separation between who builds the model and who controls the customer. A company that only makes models has to compete for a slot inside someone else's channel, and it competes on price. In the same week, Anthropic launched Haiku 5.5, an entry-level AI model up to 90% cheaper than the prior version for short requests. In July, OpenAI had already cut the price of its entry-level developer model by 80%. When the channel can switch models task by task, the model maker competes on price, so the price per use falls while multi-year contracts to buy the compute capacity behind that usage remain fixed.</p></div><div class="flags"><div class="flags-label">Visible FLAGs</div><span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> the amount Microsoft is expected to pay comes from a single report with a single source and has not been disclosed publicly. <span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> the Nvidia–Reflection talks are at an early stage and Reuters could not independently confirm them. <span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> two of the four stories involve Anthropic; this edition is written with help from Claude, an Anthropic model. <span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> the date of Musk's post varies between 10/06 and 10/07 depending on the source. <span class="flag-tag">FLAG</span> the Haiku 5.5 price reduction is an Anthropic figure; the 90% applies to requests of up to 100,000 text units; on average, after accounting for the newer m
