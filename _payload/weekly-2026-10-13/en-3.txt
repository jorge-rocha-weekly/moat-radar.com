Companies that measure success by how much gets produced will see the number rise while delivery time stays flat.</p></div><div class="label-mono">UNDERSTANDING THE BOTTLENECK</div><div class="body-text"><p>The Harvard study measures something many companies feel but cannot prove. With AI agents, work volume rises at every stage: 30% more code, 20% more change records, 23% more code-inclusion requests. But each request gets 35% more comments and nearly twice as many adjustment requests, while time to approval rises 49%. Task completion, which is what the customer actually receives, does not change in a statistically significant way, and the authors rule out gains above roughly 12%. Employment did not fall either. The gain became trapped in review.</p><p>OpenAI showed the same phenomenon outside software engineering. It released 722 manuscripts at once, many of them without independent verification, and verification came afterward: an internal audit found the error the day after publication. When an error enters a chain of results that depend on one another, it can invalidate several at once. Alex Townsend, a mathematics professor at Cornell, said he expected more errors.</p><p>Nadella describes the answer from the control side: trust the model less and the structure around it more.
