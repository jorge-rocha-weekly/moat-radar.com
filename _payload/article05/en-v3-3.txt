 house thousands of servers. Between infrastructure and hardware there is also an important software layer, with platforms such as CUDA and PyTorch helping developers use and coordinate these computing resources.</p>
<p>Next come GPUs (Graphics Processing Units, processors originally developed for graphics and now fundamental to AI), TPUs (Tensor Processing Units, specialized accelerators developed by Google) and other chips that perform the calculations.</p>
<p>HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) must feed data to these processors at enormous speed. Networks connect thousands of accelerators so they can operate as a single system.</p>
<p>Further down the chain are advanced packaging, which integrates chips and memory at high density; foundries, specialized factories that manufacture chips designed by others; the equipment required to produce those chips; and EDA (Electronic Design Automation) tools used to design and validate circuits.</p>
<p>All of this requires power and cooling.</p>
<p>AI looks like software on the surface. Underneath, it is also an enormous industrial chain.</p>

<h2 class="headline">A chain with few alternatives</h2>
<p>When we look at the complete stack, meaning the set of technological layers that make up the system, the concentration is striking.</p>
<p>In HBM, three manufacturers account for virtually the entire market. In chip manufacturing, or foundry, a single company accounts for more than 70% of the global market. In EUV (Extreme Ultraviolet) lithography, used to produce the most advanced chips, there is essentially a single supplier.<a class="footnote" href="#ref-2">[2]</a></p>
<p>This concentration did not emerge by accident.</p>
<p>Capital, intellectual property, scale, accumulated know-how, long development cycles and high switching costs have created barriers that take years, and in some cases decades, to reproduce.</p>
<p>That is why, at several points in the chain, there are very few real alternatives.</p>
<h2 class="headline">The whole system matters</h2>
<p>For much of the current AI race, attention has focused on GPUs.</p>
<p>But a more powerful GPU does not solve the problem on its own.</p>
<p>It also needs fast memory, connectivity, packaging, software, power and cooling. As clusters, groups of thousands of computers working in coordination, grow larger, the efficiency of the whole system becomes as important as the performance of any individual component.</p>
<p class="short-emphasis">The relevant unit is no longer just the chip. It becomes the system.</p>
<p>And that helps explain another phenomenon: bottlenecks move.</p>
<p>At one point, the constraint may be accelerators. Then memory or packaging. With larger clusters, networks and interconnects become more important. With denser data centers, transformers, electrical infrastructure, power availability and cooling begin to limit expansion.</p>
<p>Solving one constraint often just reveals the next one.</p>
<p>In the end, a chain that starts with extremely sophisticated algorithms comes to depend on something very basic:</p>
<p class="standalone-question">electricity.</p>
<h2 class="headline">Competition also cuts across layers</h2>
<p>There is another movement taking place inside this stack.</p>
<p>The largest players are trying to reduce their dependencies and occupy different parts of the chain.</p>
<p>Hyperscalers are developing their own chips. Model companies are moving into applications and agents. Infrastructure manufacturers are moving into systems, software and services.</p>
<p>Competition, therefore, does not happen only between companies in the same layer.</p>
<p class="short-emphasis">It also happens between the layers themselves.</p>
<p>And perhaps the most open layer of all is the one closest to the user.</p>
<p>The applications and agents layer is still being built.</p>
<p>We saw something similar after the internet and the smartphone: infrastructure and platforms were fundamental, but a huge amount of value was later created in the applications that won the customer relationship.</p>
<p>It is still too early to know whether AI will follow exactly the same path.</p>
